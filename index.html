Given an input image, a human-motion sequence (SMPL), and a prescribed camera trajectory, Directing the World generates stable long-horizon (15–20s) world-model videos with consistent human-motion control and coherent camera-based world exploration — where prior methods (Self-Forcing, Uni3C, Lingbot-World) suffer from memory degradation, scene inconsistency, or uncontrolled motion.

Abstract

Building interactive world models requires not only generating visually realistic videos, but also simulating controllable real-world dynamics over long temporal horizons. Autoregressive video generation provides a scalable foundation for such dynamic world simulation, where future observations are progressively synthesized from previously generated context. However, maintaining temporal stability over extended rollouts remains fundamentally challenging, as small prediction errors can accumulate and quickly degrade long-horizon generation. This challenge becomes even more pronounced when the model must support heterogeneous controls such as human motion and camera trajectories, since different control signals may interfere with each other and destabilize the pretrained video prior.

To address these challenges, we present Directing the World, a fast autoregressive framework for controllable world-model video generation with compositional human-motion and camera-trajectory control. Our key insight is to decouple the learning of dynamic control factors while preserving a unified autoregressive video prior. We introduce a Fast–Slow Memory training strategy that stabilizes long-horizon rollout learning and improves convergence during controllable post-training. To enable accurate and temporally smooth human-motion control, we design a t-guided Dynamic Projection mechanism together with a refined Motion-CFG strategy, which inject motion conditions adaptively across denoising stages and improve motion alignment without sacrificing visual fidelity. After acquiring a robust human-motion prior, we introduce camera-trajectory control in a second stage, allowing the model to compose human dynamics with camera motion for controllable world exploration.

Method

We propose a two-stage autoregressive ControlNet-based framework following a "decouple then integrate" principle: we first learn human-motion control under relatively static camera settings, then progressively introduce camera-trajectory control on top of the acquired motion prior. The framework contains four key components:

① MMPL Backbone

A long-horizon autoregressive backbone (Plan-then-Populate) that maintains long-range temporal consistency and world memory across extended rollouts.

② Human Motion Control

A fine-grained SMPL-based motion branch with a timestep-guided projection mechanism that adapts geometric motion conditions to timestep-dependent denoising latents. Naturally supports multi-person control.

③ Causal-Aligned Camera Control

A camera-control pathway that decouples global trajectory encoding from block-local feature injection, aligning global camera features with block-wise autoregressive generation.

④ Fast–Slow Memory Training

A differential learning-rate strategy that keeps pretrained self/cross-attention layers stable (slow memory) while letting new control modules adapt rapidly (fast memory).

Results

We evaluate on APRIL-AIGC/UltraVideo-Long under motion-only, camera-only, and joint motion-camera control settings. Dataset

We construct a large-scale controllable world-model dataset from 20M publicly available iStock videos with synchronized video, text, human-motion, and camera-trajectory annotations. A multi-stage filtering and alignment pipeline (aesthetic & quality filter, motion-aware filter, SfM camera assessment, SMPL recovery, unified-latent-space alignment) yields roughly 50k high-quality video-condition pairs, organized into:

  • Motion-centric subset (~20k) — high human motion, low camera motion; for Stage-I human-motion control.
  • Camera-centric subset (~30k) — high human motion, high camera motion; for Stage-II world-exploration camera control.

Curated datasets will be released to support future research.
